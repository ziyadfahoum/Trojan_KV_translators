KV-Lingo Reproduction

Castin, Sakamoto et al., KV-Lingo: Learning KV-Cache Translators with Distillation (arXiv 2609.32610), reproduced on the shape-preserving Qwen3-0.6B / 1.7B pair, reasoning-off, one seed, on NVIDIA B200 GPUs. All numbers below are from this run unless marked paper.

1. Headline: distilled linear maps reach the small model, closed form does not

Mean over ARC-Challenge, MMLU-Pro, CoQA (token F1) and RULER needle-in-a-haystack recall (4k/8k/16k), the analogue of the paper's Figure 3a. Each translator is evaluated in both directions: L→S means the 1.7B prefilled and the 0.6B decodes from the translated cache; S→L the reverse.

Per benchmark

Bold: best translator per column within a direction. ARC-C and MMLU-Pro are lenient generate-and-parse accuracy (MMLU-Pro macro over its 14 categories, 100 questions each); CoQA is token-F1 on one random turn per conversation with earlier turns as chat history; RULER is a synthetic needle-in-a-haystack recall averaged over 4k, 8k and 16k contexts.

Reading it against the paper

  • Closed form is not enough. Paper (Fig. 3a): closed-form translators score 0.2–0.3 against the small model's 0.5. Here: 0.32–0.43 against 0.53.
  • Distillation lifts the same linear family to the small model's level. Paper: KV-Lingo reaches the small model in both directions. Here: S→L lands within ±0.02 of the 0.6B; L→S lands above it (0.60 vs 0.53), between the two models.
  • The MLP buys nothing. Paper: linear ≈ head-wise MLP. Here: within 0.02 in both directions, and the MLP extrapolates worse on RULER at 16k.
  • Head-wise suffices on released Qwen3 pairs (aligned heads, paper App. G): here head-wise trails head-mixing by only 0.02–0.03 with 8× fewer parameters.
  • L→S hands part of the large model's representation to the small one. The 0.6B decoding from the translated 1.7B cache scores 0.717 on ARC-C, above the 1.7B's own 0.683, and its held-out perplexity falls from 7.18 to 6.71. The paper reports L→S matching the large model on easy tasks (Fig. 10).

2. Distillation objective: held-out KL across training

KL( ptarget(· | own cache) ‖ ptarget(· | translated cache) ) on 64 held-out Nemotron conversations, measured every 250 steps. Step 0 is the closed-form initialisation (the MLP starts from its MSE-trained stage 1).

3. Why linear maps work: cache geometry (paper Appendix B)

R² is the held-out variance of the target's cache explained by the closed-form linear map, averaged over layers. Effective rank is exp(entropy) of the per-layer key covariance spectrum (of 1024 dimensions).

4. Switch cost: translation + one step vs. re-prefill

Time to first token after a model switch on one B200, Hugging Face Transformers, bf16, batch 1 (the paper's Table 36 setting). Translation is a token-wise linear map and grows linearly with the prefix; re-prefill grows quadratically.

7. Beyond the paper: why S→L caps at the small model, and what fixes it

Three attempts to push S→L above the small model, then a diagnosis, then the fix. Numbers are reasoning-off, one seed, Qwen3-0.6B → 1.7B.

Diagnosis: the prefiller's reading of the prompt decides the answer

On ARC-C the 1.7B decoding from the translated 0.6B cache agrees with the 0.6B's answers 75% of the time and with its own native answers only 59%; on the 284 questions only the 1.7B answers correctly it scores 30%. The mirror holds for L→S (82% on those questions). The cache carries the prefiller's understanding of the question, and the decoder mostly reads it out.

What does not work

Prefill cost: fraction of a full target prefill spent on recomputation. The hidden-state signal is identical to K/V for S→L by construction (the 0.6B's K and V are invertible linear images of its layer input). Every variant lowers the held-out KL, none moves ARC: lower KL is not a reliable proxy for benchmarks here.

What works: translate the context, let the decoder read the query itself

The target runs its full stack natively over only the last K prompt tokens, attending to the translated context (the paper's Appendix T ablation, here on an unbalanced pair and on knowledge tasks). No retraining.

ARC prompts are about 100 tokens, so at K ≥ 128 the whole question is native and only the chat header is translated. The remaining gap is on context-grounded tasks, where the translated context still costs a few points. Retraining the translator under this wrapper lowered KL further but did not improve benchmarks. In the L→S direction the same wrapper lowers ARC from 0.717 to 0.549, the 0.6B's own level: the query's processor decides, whichever model it is.

Alignment follows the same rule

StrongREJECT, 200 prompts, judge gpt-4.1-mini. ASR = share of prompts not refused; compliance = not refused and judged convincing and specific (StrongREJECT score ≥ 0.5).

Translation raises the 1.7B's compliance from 18.5% to 25.5% (paired McNemar p = 0.024; stable under re-judging with gpt-4.1-mini and gpt-4.1). Native query prefill brings it back to 17.5% (vs translation, p = 0.007). The 0.6B does not inherit the 1.7B's refusals from its cache in L→S (p = 0.53).

Switch cost of query prefill

HF stack, B200, batch 1. The query prefill costs one extra forward over the cache; in this harness that cost does not depend on K between 64 and 1024, so it is call overhead rather than the query tokens.

6. What was reproduced, what was changed

  • Pair and scope. Only the shape-preserving Qwen3-0.6B/1.7B pair (28 layers, 8 KV heads × 128). No cross-layer, Gemma, cross-tokenizer, MLA or MoE experiments. Reasoning-off only, one seed (paper: three seeds on this pair).
  • Method as in the paper. Pre-k-norm capture; split key/value maps; head-mixing and head-wise linear translators; closed-form stage 1 via normal equations in fp64 with a pseudo-inverse; stage 2 self-distillation with the forward KL from the target's own distribution; the last prefix token is processed by the target. A parity test with an identity translator reproduces the target's native logits and greedy generations exactly.
  • Training data. Nemotron-SFT-Instruction-Following-Chat-v2, natural plus packed samples, as in Appendix C. The released reasoning_on split carries no think traces, so every sample is reasoning-off. Prefix cap 8k instead of 16k; 2000 steps instead of 5000; learning rates taken from the paper's Table 5 without a sweep.
  • Evaluation. Own prompts and lenient parsers; no judge-based benchmarks (MT-Bench-101, RepLiQA, LongMemEval); RULER is a self-built needle task on Nemotron text rather than the official suite; CoQA uses one random turn per conversation.
  • Timing. HF stack only; no vLLM serving numbers.
